# TV numerical implementation notes

These retained technical notes describe the existing solver checks, cusp references, limits, and reuse rules. For the file map and routine maintenance, start with [README.md](README.md).

## Graph lab and rational references

The Graphs page integrates the former graph correspondence preview. It illustrates base-core pair insertions for all three TV seeds, up to 3 pairs per chain and 16 hemispheres. Explore retains its larger count range and full admissible decoration controls. Incoming unsupported graph configurations are identified explicitly by the lab, rather than silently drawn as though they matched. The link from the lab opens the actual displayed case in Explore.

The highlighted cusp fractions are **proposed references for simulation**. Where the graph model supplies a fraction, the page labels it as a graph proposal. Where no count formula is available, a tentative fraction may be reconstructed from a converging computed tail, with a bounded denominator and stability checks. Until those checks pass, the candidate remains unresolved. A closer numerical match is evidence, not a proof. All curves still consist of solved points, and no proposed fraction is inserted as a cusp endpoint.

Explicit conjectures now cover all tetrahedral mixed pair-insertion counts, all admissible double-edge arm/cross/connector/loop combinations, and the core-dependent three-loop affine rows. Limits & Zoom displays the applicable formula with the current counts. Mathematics defines the labels, charts, and conjectural logarithmic rates, and compares finite computed points with the proposed limits. Double-edge cusp traces now use adaptive marked-graph continuation with healthy independent Hodge meshes, bounded corrections and repeated-precision checks. The validated cancellation-free infinity solver remains for pure connectors. Double-edge vertex paths retain their separate numerical approach. Conjectural formulas are annotations only and are never imported by the root solvers. Older double-edge numerical caches are separated by a numerical revision; display settings and other TV-family caches are retained.


## Tetrahedral solver and cusp references

Tetrahedral open-edge traces use `numerics/tetra-solver.mjs`: local predictor-corrector continuation, embedded half-step and reverse checks, independent healthy graph checkpoints, and perturbed higher-precision repeats. An unresolved graph audit is reported as inconclusive, with its checked prefix retained. Depth extension reuses checked points; no theoretical endpoint is inserted. The validated cancellation-free infinity path remains for pure connectors, and source-vertex paths keep their separate numerical route.

Mixed tetrahedral corrections use guarded fixed-point Fourier arithmetic in `numerics/tetra-fixed-corrector.mjs`, with the original Decimal corrector as fallback. Correction targets, local bounds, half-step and reverse checks, and displayed-point precision repeats are unchanged. Independent graph selection still uses Decimal arithmetic. For calibrated maximum weights 4–8, cone-centered physical meshes reduce initialization cost while retaining two healthy independent meshes and the original 30-digit refinement target. Existing checked results and display settings remain compatible.

Compact tetrahedral continuation uses a locally fixed Schur residual to avoid the poorly scaled product of all determinant pivots. The same row/column normalization and pivot order are used throughout each local correction; unsafe leading pivots trigger the existing higher-precision fallback. Exact zero, unit and axis arithmetic shortcuts reduce redundant fixed-point work. The source grid, connectivity, reverse checks and perturbed endpoint precision repeats are unchanged. Progress updates arrive every four checked mesh points. This speeds up mixed interior images without lowering their resolution or changing saved-case compatibility; the existing time allowance and resumable partial-result behavior remain in place for larger requests.

Dense compact images also reuse exact phase and derivative coefficients. Within a bounded compact range (Fourier degree at most 18, reduced height between 0.8 and 8, and imaginary theta argument no larger than that height), combined Fourier amplitudes advance by a recurrence. Outside those guards, each call uses the stable direct evaluator. Compact Lamé-factor endpoints use the guarded arithmetic too; independent anchors and cusp traces retain their direct evaluation. Both 24/34-digit endpoint correction targets, the perturbed precision repeat, full residual checks and the original Decimal fallback remain in place.

On supported multicore devices, tetrahedral images with at least 45 source points use two or three child workers, with a lower cap when a low-memory hint is available. Each child independently selects its marked anchor and retains all continuation and endpoint checks. Checked samples are merged back into the original grid and connectivity. All children share the original 45-second wall deadline and terminate when the request ends; constructor failure permits serial fallback only within the remaining allowance. Unsupported devices use the faster serial path. Numerical caches and display settings are compatible with previous checked results. Completion time still depends on the device, counts and resolution.

Equal counts on each pair of opposite labelled edges use the marked multiplication-by-two Lamé reduction, with separate character lifts for every `(a,b,c)`. The included classical anchor table covers `a+b+c <= 20` (index `n <= 21`); other configurations use the full TV selector within the existing numerical limits. Compact images reuse one selected anchor and short bounded continuations, rather than constructing a graph mesh for every displayed vertex. Internal steps use a bounded binary64 corrector with frozen local normalization and reverse checks; unsafe steps fall back to higher precision. Every displayed endpoint still receives perturbed corrections at two higher working precisions.

For all six tetrahedral counts, Limits & Zoom now gives the proposed rational horizontal limit, logarithmic rate `2/M`, and positive leading constant from two cap residues. Mathematics defines the full formula and its current numerical evidence. Constants are computed with exact integer products and logarithms to avoid overflow. These remain conjectural annotations and never enter root initialization. Each family's numerical revision is independent; display preferences, other family results, and the shared Explore/Limits cache are retained.

## Three-loop solver and cusp comparisons

Three-loop points, compact images, and open-edge traces now use the repaired marked-graph solver. Cone-centered Hodge meshes select the initial branch. Bounded continuation and perturbed precision checks follow it; combined Fourier exponents avoid the former division-by-zero error in the trapped-component case. The numerical worker does not import conjecture annotations. Ordinary plotted roots use an 18-digit correction target with adaptive working precision; the much higher research precision is not a simulation default.

Limits & Zoom gives the complete marked fraction `P/(2M)`, logarithmic rate `1/M`, and rational cap-residue constant `K` for the chosen graph. The comparison uses the actual edge distance and normalized boundary position, including the factor `sin(pi*t)`. The evidence area compares the computed constant with the proposal at the available plotting precision. Mathematics defines these quantities and gives mixed-decoration examples, including negative fractions, fractions greater than one, and trapped hemispheres. Large resonance can make convergence slow even at a very small epsilon.

The new numerical revision applies only to three-loop computed results. Existing display preferences and other families' results are retained. New checked points remain shared between Explore and Limits; increasing depth continues compatible saved traces, and a time stop retains a resumable checkpoint while its worker is available. Keep all files in `numerics/` when uploading this folder to GitHub Pages; there is still no build step or numerical backend.

## Overall simulation checks

Dense double-edge and three-loop interiors use the same bounded worker pool as the tetrahedral images when nested workers are supported. The original source grid, marked branch, numerical limits and one shared 45-second deadline are retained. Child failure or a time stop preserves only the checked points received so far; all child workers terminate when the request ends.

Double-edge compact transport uses guarded Fourier arithmetic, followed by the existing independent Decimal endpoint correction and its stronger fallback. Exact phase/binomial caches and a same-source fast prepolish remove repeated work while retaining the correction targets and trust bounds. Cusp traces and standard point computations retain their existing numerical route.

Three-loop compact endpoints use two fresh bounded binary64 matching models and an error check in all four displayed coordinates. The correction target is 3e-12 and the plot agreement threshold is 1e-9, strengthening the former preview checks. An unresolved, ill-conditioned or disagreeing model escalates to separate 18/28-digit corrections with a fresh perturbed higher-precision solve. Higher working precision, stable scaled arithmetic and Decimal fallbacks remain available. Standard point readouts and cusp traces retain their full checks. Geometry still requires independent healthy marked Hodge meshes and the existing root-agreement tests. These remain numerical estimates, not interval certificates.

Checked canonical base-image samples can be reused across decorations within each of these two seed families, including between Explore and Limits & Zoom. A versioned base artifact records the actual source grid, inset, precision and check metadata. The worker admits only exact source matches; missing or incompatible samples use the normal computation. Selected decorated roots are always computed for the selected graph. Base artifacts share the existing 16 MB / 32-entry result cache and its eviction policy; there is no additional unbounded database. Trace depth reuse and display preferences retain their existing keys.

The two `verified-base-*.mjs` data files supply the common 45/91-point base grids on the first visit too. All 272 bundled base roots were independently checked with the full unfactored equation at 160-digit working precision, in all four display charts. They contain no decorated periods or ideal cusp endpoints. Only the selected family's file is loaded, only for compact images; a missing, incompatible or unsupported grid falls back to computation. Keep these data files when uploading the folder. Their numerical certificates and provenance are included in each module.

Point requests respect their computation budget and report actual elapsed time. Double-edge compact images compare transported iterates with a bounded decimal correction; released points receive repeated-precision checks. This keeps mesh work practical while supplying coordinate diagnostics. The tetrahedral seed shares its identical base and selected continuation. Exhausted precision is reported separately from a rejected graph.

Double-edge and tetrahedral cases with maximum weight above 12 use cone-centered Hodge meshes for initialization. Independent healthy meshes must still agree after bounded root correction; the trust radius and marking checks are retained. Only these large-count numerical caches receive a new revision. Smaller cases, the three-loop cache, and display preferences are retained.

Compatible checked trace prefixes also survive a page or family change: continue an unfinished trace at the same target, or increase its depth. The worker rechecks the last root before continuing. Changed graphs, paths or sampling settings, rejected branches and separated unchecked tails cannot reuse a prefix. Existing display preferences and complete cached results are preserved.

The allowed count range is a capacity limit, not a promise that every mixed case finishes quickly. Larger graphs start with a small compact mesh and a short tail, with enough time for graph initialization. Difficult runs keep checked partial data. Compare consecutive real parts and the leading-constant ratio before interpreting a proposed asymptotic; large resonance can delay that regime well beyond the default preview.

## Slower-device checks and unfinished images

Partial compact interiors now continue across runs, pages and family switches. Explore's **Compute interior image** and Limits & Zoom's **Compute approach** reuse checked vertices of the exact same labelled graph, actual source grid and inset. Their stored period strings are unchanged. Missing vertices still select their marked anchor and pass the usual continuation and endpoint checks; saved roots do not initialize a new branch. Mismatched graphs, grids, sources or incomplete accuracy metadata are rejected for reuse and computed normally. A finer partial image from Explore keeps its grid when continued in Limits & Zoom. Complete cached images retain the previous instant-restore behaviour and memory budget.

Nested module workers can be unavailable even when the browser exposes `Worker`. Constructor or asynchronous startup failure now permits serial fallback within the original shared deadline if no new checked vertices arrived. Cancellation, the time limit and numerical rejection do not trigger a restart. Explore uses one cusp worker when the browser reports at most 2 GB of device memory; failed replacement workers settle their pending series and leave the controls usable.

The simulation audit includes larger mixed graphs, a count-32 double-edge connector, a high-order tetrahedral Lamé reduction, independent full-equation precision checks, and actual Windows processor-affinity tests on one and two logical CPUs. Affinity constrains CPU access; it does not reproduce a slower processor or every mobile device. Some very large mixed double-edge and tetrahedral images still need multiple 45-second runs. Checked partial vertices now accumulate instead of being recomputed. No accuracy threshold, numerical branch check or source resolution was reduced for this fallback.
