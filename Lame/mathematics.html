CLASSICAL LAMÉ · MATHEMATICAL BACKGROUND

From hemisphere attachments to conformal images

The one-cone Lamé family: three counts, four character cells, and a marked conformal image with computed cusp ends. Compare one attachment in Explore, a boundary approach in Limits & Zoom, or every attachment of one order in the Overlap Atlas.

1. A spherical torus and the Lamé equation

A hemisphere gluing gives a metric of curvature 1 away from its conical point. Its marked conformal torus has periods 1 and τ, with Im τ > 0. Here the order is a positive integer n, and the unique cone angle is (4n + 2)π.

Eτ=ℂ/(ℤ+τℤ),y″=[n(n+1)℘(z;τ)+B]y

The accessory parameter B selects the differential equation at this modulus. The atlas follows the equation and its marked geometric branch through the gluing data. It does not treat the cone angle, the modulus, and the monodromy as independent choices.

2. The three attachment counts

Choose nonnegative integers a, b, c. They count hemisphere attachments along the three labelled edges of the base spherical triangle. The initial triangle has angles π, π, π. The resulting angles and order are

(α1,α2,α3)=(1+b+c,1+a+c,1+a+b)πn=1+a+b+c,area=4πn

A zero count collapses its arm. Every bridge has both incidences in F₀. Thus only F₀ gains degree, and the face weights are (n,0,0,0); F₁, F₂, F₃ remain smooth marked points upstairs. A dot is a hemisphere vertex, not a conical point. The drawing records topology, not spherical lengths.

Supported range. Explore and Limits & Zoom accept a + b + c ≤ 20, hence 1 ≤ n ≤ 21. This is a computation limit. The graph changes immediately when a count is edited; applying the decoration selects the corresponding numerical case.

3. Source triangle and character shifts

The lower-case coordinates r, s are the analytic character coordinates used throughout this Lamé atlas. Their open source triangle is

Δ={(r,s):0<r,s<12,r+s>12}

The displayed boundary is dashed and excluded. Dragging takes place in a compact inset; cusp paths approach an open edge without reaching it. For an attachment (a,b,c), the analytic parameters become

u=r+a+c2,v=s+b+c2

With the chosen period basis and eigenline, write the ordinary eigencharacter as

χ(1)=e−2πiv,χ(τ)=e2πiu

Changing eigenline simultaneously inverts both multipliers. For a compatible ratio f of two solutions, the developing map satisfies

f(z+ω)=χ(ω)2f(z),ω∈ℤ+τℤ

The half-integer shifts change ordinary signs, while their squares leave projective monodromy unchanged. Their parities give the four ordinary character cells shown in Explore. The atlas places these cells using integer translations and simultaneous inversion; this rearrangement does not translate the conformal image.

The signs require the chosen linear equation, an eigenline, and the lift convention above. The projective gluing alone determines only the squared multipliers. The full labelled attachment and transported period marking select the conformal branch.

4. The equation used to find τ

At modulus τ, let η₁ and η₂ be the quasi-periods of the Weierstrass zeta function along 1 and τ. Put

α=u+vτ,Z=ζ(α;τ)−uη1−vη2x=℘(α;τ),y=℘′(α;τ)

The order-n premodular residual has weight n(n + 1)/2. Its first two cases are

P1=Z,P2=Z3−3xZ−y

The worker uses a recurrence for higher orders and solves its vanishing equation at the shifted parameters (u,v). Large orders use a Bloch-jet Schur predictor followed by higher-precision correction with the premodular recurrence. A nonzero normalization controls the size of intermediate values without changing the roots.

A root is not a branch label. The numerical root is selected by the labelled gluing and its marked period anchor, then transported by continuation. An unrestricted zero search can reach a different surface with the same character.

5. Compact images and cusp tails

For each ordered triple, the selected marked branch gives a map from Δ into the upper half-plane. Explore compares the n = 1 image with the chosen attachment. Its shaded interior is the image of a sampled compact source inset, not the entire domain. The conformal boundary is not obtained by joining three vertex values with straight segments.

Horizontal limits in a cusp coordinate

The existing Lamé convention uses the following rational reference for the infinity edge:

h(a,b,c)=2c+12(b+c+1)

The three reference values in the table order are h(a,b,c), h(c,a,b), and h(b,c,a). These are limits of Re w in the stated charts. In particular, the latter two are not limits of Re τ. For the base case each value is ½.

Status of this reference. The inherited Lamé implementation marks the decorated rational line as a prediction checked against independently computed traces. This page does not promote those finite computations to an all-count theorem or assert a new leading-error bound. The line is a comparison target; every plotted point is obtained from the full period equation.

Limits & Zoom keeps the original marked overview alongside a local cusp chart. Its two local plots compare equal coordinate units with a separately magnified horizontal scale. Absolute real coordinates and the tiny displacement from a reference convey different information; the trace retains decimal values until drawing offsets are formed.

6. Comparing every attachment of one order

The Overlap Atlas computes the ordered triples satisfying a + b + c = n − 1. There are n(n + 1)/2 such triples. It places their compact images and six cusp approaches in a shared picture, with a selected-case and a gallery option.

Original positions and intersections

The Γ(2) overview uses the original τ coordinate with linear axes and equal coordinate units. A common disk or common cusp chart applies the same injective map to every image and preserves their intersections.

Comparing shapes

Separately centered affine and disk views place a different coordinate map on each attachment. They can equalize the visible size of small images, but their apparent intersections do not represent intersections in the original τ-plane.

The common disk uses a fixed upper-half-plane point τ₀ for all attachments:

ζ=τ−τ0τ−τ0¯,τ0=12+32i

This is a change of complex coordinate, not a compression of the imaginary axis in the original overview. Exponential cusp coordinates identify points differing by their period; they preserve intersections only within an appropriate strip.

Triangle tint controls the light fill of each sampled image. Overlap darkness changes only regions covered by at least two distinct images. An attachment’s interior and tail ribbon are counted as one image. These controls change the drawing and do not rerun the solver.

Dashed curves interpolate computed contours smoothly. The exact boundary, overlap multiplicity outside the sampled region, and unresolved cusp portions are not certified by the drawing.

7. Numerical checks, speed, and reuse

  1. Select the marked branch. Retain the ordered counts, character lift, and geometric period anchor.
  2. Continue from a resolved point. A nearby solved period predicts the next root; unstable steps are subdivided instead of drawing across them.
  3. Correct at higher precision. Deep cusp traces use stable Fourier evaluation and decimal correction. Repeated precision checks compare the coordinates and, when available, the small horizontal displacement relative to its reference.
  4. Cache actual computed points. Explore and Limits & Zoom can reuse matching results. Increasing the depth extends a compatible trace from its retained samples. The all-attachments view has its own bounded local cache and symmetry reuse.
  5. Keep partial output honest. A failed precision, branch, or time check leaves the earlier samples available and reports the reason. A partial trace does not imply that the geometric surface ceases to exist.

Computation runs in browser workers on the visitor’s device. The site is static and needs no paid remote computation service. Larger orders and deeper tails cost more time; the recommended settings give a first picture before requesting finer comparisons.

What a numerical plot establishes. Agreement of residuals, repeated precision, and independent branch checks are numerical evidence. They are not interval enclosures, proofs of global injectivity, or a certification of the complete boundary. Smooth interpolation improves readability without adding mathematical samples.
