Computation & caching

Tails are computed for every image, not replaced by arrows or forced endpoints. The overview keeps their true positions; the comparison uses the coordinates chosen below.

At large orders, computing the full atlas for the first time can take several minutes. Images appear as they finish. Selecting an attachment prioritizes its queued computation; Stop keeps checked samples for the next run.

Computed points are saved locally and reused when available.

Coordinate details and numerical limits

Click an image or attachment triple to emphasize it. Click empty plot or page space to clear the emphasis. The active attachment and its computed data are retained, including in the selected-image view.

The common disk applies one coordinate to every attachment, preserving their relative positions and overlaps:

ζ=τ−τ0τ−τ0¯,τ0=12+32i.

For the centered comparisons, the source center determines τ*=Fabc(13,13). The affine option applies only a translation and a positive real scaling:

z=τ−τ*Imτ*Equal interior scale instead uses the sampled interior root-mean-square radius. Tails do not set this scale.

Centering each branch changes their relative positions. Actual overlaps should be read in the original τ-plane or in a common cusp chart. The cusp charts send the chosen cusp to infinity; their local coordinate q=eπiw sends it to zero and also identifies points differing by its period.

Two source paths approach each cusp down to the chosen ε. Computed transition points connect them to the compact interior. Smooth dashed curves interpolate the resolved samples; they do not add numerical roots or certify a boundary. Ribbons are not certified enclosures of the full image. Sample markers are off by default and can be enabled separately. No finite endpoint stands for infinity. Stopping preserves the samples already resolved. Display changes reuse those samples.
